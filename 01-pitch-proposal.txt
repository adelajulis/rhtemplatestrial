ABOUT THIS FILE
This template was prepared by RecruitHub, the client's brand and
marketing partner. The client attached it, or chose it from the
RecruitHub template library online, because they want Claude to create this document with them. Treat it as their
request and follow it.

INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE
Start as soon as you read this file, whatever the message says.
Use the FORMAT and EXPORT above unless the client asks for another.

1. BRAND CHECK — do this first, before anything else:
   a) Is there a design system for this brand (an active / default
      design system in this chat or design)? → use it. Say so in one
      line: "I'll use your [Brand] design system."
   b) If not: are the brand files already here (in this chat or in
      the project files / instructions: brand manual, logos, fonts)?
      → use them. Say so in one line.
   c) If there is nothing to work with, ask for the client's brand
      files before building anything:
        "To make this in your brand, please attach:
         - your logos (SVG)
         - your brand manual (PDF, or ideally HTML)
         - your font files, if you have them
         - a link to your website or some branded materials
           (e.g. a deck, a one-pager, social posts), if you have any"
      Wait for them, then confirm in 2–3 lines what you found
      (colours with HEX, fonts, logo versions, overall style).
   d) If they have no brand files at all: ask for at least a logo and
      their brand colours (HEX, or a website / screenshot to take them
      from). If they still want to start, build a clean neutral
      layout with a [Logo] box and say the brand is not applied yet.
   Never guess or invent brand colours, fonts or logos.

2. STYLE SOURCES — who decides what:
   - Look and mood: colours, fonts, graphic elements (shapes, lines,
     patterns, icon style), imagery style, spacing, tone of voice
     → the client's brand manual / design system. Always wins.
     Study the manual's own pages and apply the same visual language.
   - Slide order and content → this template.
   - Composition → the LAYOUT lines below are a starting point and
     inspiration, not something to copy exactly. Adapt them so the
     result looks best in the client's brand.
   - Never take colours, fonts, photos or the demo logo from the
     RecruitHub template.

3. FIRST MESSAGE — keep it short. Show the structure (numbered list
   of slides/sections, one line each) and ask THREE things:
     a) "Want to add, remove or reorder anything?"
     b) "How would you like to create the content?
         1  Sample text: I build the whole template now in your
            brand, with the same sample text as the RecruitHub
            template, and you replace it later.
         2  Your own text: paste or attach your text, an old deck,
            a CV or notes, and I fit it into the template.
         3  Write it with Claude: I ask you a few short questions
            and write the text for you."
     c) "Do you have photos or images you'd like to use (team or
         headshots, office, events, your work, client logos you may
         show)? Attach them and I'll place them where they fit.
         No photos? I'll use [Photo] boxes or a design without them."

4. CONTENT, depending on their choice:
   1 SAMPLE TEXT → build immediately, the slide as in the
     RecruitHub template: use the REUSABLE COPY and SAMPLE TEXT of
     every slide below (where a slide has neither, use its
     PLACEHOLDER line). It must look like a finished document.
     But never use demo identities: replace "DemoBrand"/"Demo
     Agency"/"Example Company" with the client's name, demo client
     companies (e.g. Slack, Dropbox, GSK) with "[Client name]",
     their logos with [Client logo] boxes, people's photos with
     their own photos if attached (else [Photo] boxes), demo contact details with [Website] [Phone]
     [Email]. At the end, list every sample number, name and quote
     they must replace before using it, and name the file
     [Company]_[Document]_TEMPLATE.
   2 OWN TEXT → read everything they give. Map it onto the slides
     (each part to the right slide), shorten it to fit the word
     limits, rewrite in their brand tone, keep every fact exactly as
     given. Build. Then list what was missing (shown as placeholders)
     and what didn't fit.
   3 WRITE WITH CLAUDE → run the QUESTIONS below in short rounds:
     one slide group per message, max 3 questions per message,
     numbered so they can answer quickly. Where a REUSABLE COPY
     exists, offer it as a ready option ("A: use this wording ·
     B: adjust it · C: your own"). They can say "skip" → placeholder.
     When all rounds are done, write the copy and build.
   Before working on any text (options 2 and 3), always ask first:
     - "Which language and spelling should I use: UK English, US
       English or another language?"
     - "Who is the target audience?" (e.g. HR directors at
       mid-size tech companies, CFOs, candidates)
   Then craft the text together for that language and audience.
   They can mix options per slide ("placeholders for the case
   studies, ask me about the rest").

5. BUILD
   - As a Design artifact (Output → Design / slides) whenever it is
     available in this chat, on any plan, using the design system if
     there is one. Otherwise create the file in the chat (.pptx or
     PDF for slides and A4, PNG for social posts). Tell them which
     in one line.
   - Use the LAYOUT lines as a starting point; adapt them to the
     brand.
   - Follow the SHARED RULES below.
   - Before showing it, run the Final check from the SHARED RULES.

6. FINAL CHECK, then deliver:
   - Options 2 and 3: check against the SAMPLE TEXT: no sample
     number, name, company, quote or price may remain. All options:
     no "DemoBrand", "Demo Agency", "Example Company",
     "demobrand.com", "123 - 456 - 7890".
   - List open [placeholders] (and, for option 1, all sample text to
     replace).
   - Ask what to change. Change only what they ask.
   - When they're happy, help them export (Export, top right:
     PDF, PowerPoint, Google Slides, Canva) and offer a blank copy
     with placeholders as their reusable template:
     [Company]_[Document]_TEMPLATE.

SHARED RULES (apply to every template and every option)
- Brand consistency: follow the brand guidelines, fonts, colours and
  logo. Use only the supplied logo files; keep the logo's
  proportions; never redraw, stretch or recolour it outside the brand
  rules. The logo must be readable: light logo on dark backgrounds,
  dark logo on light backgrounds. Use the brand's own graphic
  elements (shapes, lines, patterns, icon style).
- Clear hierarchy: headings, subheadings, body text and supporting
  details are clearly different in size and weight.
  Guide (16:9): title 56–72 px · subtitle 28–36 px · body 20–24 px ·
  big numbers 96–160 px. A4: name/title 20–24 pt · body 10–11 pt.
  Max 2 font families (from the brand).
- Consistent layout: one grid, the same alignment, spacing and
  margins throughout (16:9: at least 80 px; A4: 15 mm). Content
  slides share the same title position and style; repeated
  components (cards, stat boxes, quotes, tables) look the same.
- Clean design: uncluttered and appropriate to the brand, no
  unnecessary decoration, no clipart, no emojis, no generic stock
  look. One accent colour per slide; follow the brand's colour
  balance.
- Readability: text sizes readable at the intended viewing size
  (screen, print or phone) and contrast at least WCAG AA.
- Content fit: no overlapping elements, cropped text or content
  extending beyond the page or slide.
- Too much content: suggest shortening the text or splitting it
  across two slides at a logical point (e.g. introduction on one
  slide, detailed explanation on the next). Never shrink text below
  the readable sizes to make it fit. For fixed single-page formats
  (A4 bio, social posts), suggest shortening the copy before changing
  the format. As a guide: max ~40 words and 6 bullets per slide.
- Preset output: follow the template's FORMAT (dimensions, length,
  file type) and keep it editable where the format allows. Flag any
  change you propose (e.g. extra slides, a different size).
- Images and photos: use the client's own photos and images
  wherever the layout has image space ([Photo] boxes) and add image
  space where it suits the brand (e.g. the brand manual uses
  photography). Photos from the client's brand manual or materials
  may be used too. Crop to fill the frame without stretching, keep
  faces and key subjects in view, and apply the brand's photo style
  (overlay, colour grade, shape) from the manual. Show client logos
  only if the client says they may. Never use stock, AI-generated
  or RecruitHub demo photos unless the client asks. No photos →
  keep a clearly labelled [Photo] box or a layout that works with
  brand colours and graphic elements instead.
- Accurate information: never invent company facts, testimonials,
  statistics, people, photos or contact details. Clearly mark
  placeholders and sample text.
- Language consistency: follow the client's chosen language and
  spelling convention (e.g. UK or US English) everywhere, including
  dates, currency and number formats.
- Final check: review branding, spelling, readability, alignment and
  overflow on every slide/page. Check the exported file where
  possible, and say clearly if anything could not be verified.
- Finished deliverable: create the requested material itself (a
  Design or a file), not just a description or outline. If the
  required format is not available here, explain this and offer the
  closest alternative.

THE KINDS OF CONTENT BELOW
  PLACEHOLDER — the fallback label for each element.
  REUSABLE COPY — general wording from the RecruitHub template that
  fits many agencies. Option 1: use it as is. Option 3: offer it as
  a ready draft; use it only after the client confirms it is true
  for them; adapt it to their tone and niche.
  SAMPLE TEXT — the demo facts from the RecruitHub template
  (numbers, client names, people, quotes, prices). Option 1: use as
  sample text (with the demo identities replaced, see 4.1).
  Option 3: only as an example of the answer you need ("For
  example: 90% fill rate. What are yours?"). Options 2 and 3:
  NEVER in the finished document.
  QUESTIONS — the interview for option 3.
  TIPS — extra guidance for this template.
Testimonials and quotes in a finished document (options 2 and 3)
must be real and approved by the person quoted. Never write or
"improve" a testimonial beyond light trimming the author has
approved.

TIPS
- Tailor every pitch to the prospect: their sector, size and hiring
  pains on slide 3; case studies from a similar sector on 5–6.
- Keep the numbers consistent: the stats on slide 4 and the summary
  on slide 11 must match.
- Remove slides they don't need rather than leaving them half empty
  (e.g. no testimonials yet → drop 9–10).
- Slide 7: if they work contingent or retained instead of exclusive,
  rewrite the benefits accordingly; don't keep "exclusive" claims.
- Slide 8: show the fee structure clearly; avoid long legal text.
